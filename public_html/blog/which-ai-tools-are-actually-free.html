Which AI Tools Are Actually Free to Use?

Guide · Jul 15, 2026

"Free" gets used loosely in AI tools. A free trial that ends, a free tier with real but limited daily use, and a genuinely free product are three different deals, and it's rarely obvious which one you're signing up for until you're a few sessions in.

A useful way to sort it is by category, since the economics are different for each.

Chat and writing tools tend to be the most generous. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok all offer some form of free chat access, with paid plans mainly buying you priority speed, longer context, or the newest model rather than being required to use the tool at all. DeepSeek is a bit different again: its models are open-weight, so "free" isn't limited to whatever a hosted free tier allows, since the underlying model can be run elsewhere too.

Image and video generation tools are usually stingier, because every generation costs real compute on the provider's side. Expect a small batch of free credits to test a tool, then a subscription or pay-as-you-go credits for anything beyond that — the free tier here is a demo, not a substitute for the paid product.

Coding assistants often sit in between: a genuinely usable free plan for individual use, with paid tiers unlocking usage limits, more capable models, or team features.

Exact limits shift often enough that any specific number we wrote today would likely be stale in a few months, so the more durable habit is this: treat every "free" claim as a tier, not a promise, and actually use the free allowance before deciding whether the paid version is worth it. That's the same philosophy behind how we built PromptuPatch's own free credits — enough real use to judge the product honestly, no artificial nagging along the way.
